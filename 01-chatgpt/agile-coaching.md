# Agile Coaching Experiment: Product Owner Changes Priorities During the Sprint

## 📌 1. Scenario:

A Scrum Team works in two-week Sprints.

The Product Owner frequently changes priorities during the Sprint and asks Developers to stop their current work and start higher-priority items.

The Scrum Master needs to understand the situation, identify possible causes and risks, and determine an appropriate coaching approach.

## 🎯 2. Objective:

Explore how ChatGPT can assist a Scrum Master in:

- Understanding the possible reasons behind frequent priority changes
- Identifying risks to the Sprint Goal
- Understanding the impact on the Scrum Team
- Preparing effective coaching questions
- Exploring possible facilitation approaches

The objective is not to blindly follow AI recommendations, but to evaluate them using Scrum principles and Scrum Master judgment.

## 🤖 3. AI Prompt — Situation Analysis:

> Act as an experienced Agile Coach and Scrum Master.
>
> A Scrum Team works in two-week Sprints. The Product Owner frequently changes priorities during the Sprint and asks Developers to stop their current work and start higher-priority items.
>
> Analyze this situation from a Scrum perspective.
>
> Identify:
> 1. Possible underlying reasons for this behavior
> 2. Risks to the Sprint Goal
> 3. Impact on Developers and the Scrum Team
> 4. Questions the Scrum Master should ask before taking action
> 5. What the Scrum Master should avoid doing
>
> Do not immediately assume that the Product Owner is behaving incorrectly. Consider different possible contexts.

## 💬 4. AI Response — Situation Analysis:
This is a **situational Scrum problem**, not automatically a Product Owner problem. The Scrum Master should first understand *why* priorities are changing and whether the Sprint Goal is still valid.

### 1. Possible underlying reasons

- **Genuine business urgency** — customer issue, regulatory change, market opportunity, production incident, etc.
- **New information** — the PO may have learned something that materially changes product priorities.
- **Unclear Sprint Goal** — if the Sprint is treated as a collection of individual stories rather than a coherent objective, reprioritization becomes easier.
- **Weak Product Backlog management** — important items may not have been sufficiently refined or ordered before Sprint Planning.
- **External pressure** — stakeholders or management may be pushing the PO to get specific work done immediately.
- **Misunderstanding of Scrum** — the PO may believe they can freely redirect Developers because they own backlog ordering.
- **Poor communication** — the PO and Developers may not have a shared understanding of what can change during a Sprint.

### 2. Risks to the Sprint Goal:

Frequent interruptions can:

- Put the **Sprint Goal at risk**
- Increase context switching and unfinished work
- Reduce focus and predictability
- Create technical debt or rushed implementation
- Make Sprint Review outcomes less meaningful
- Encourage Developers to optimize for individual requests rather than the Sprint Goal

However, an important Scrum nuance is that **scope can be clarified and renegotiated during a Sprint without endangering the Sprint Goal**. If the Sprint Goal genuinely becomes obsolete, the PO can even cancel the Sprint.

### 3. Impact on Developers and the team:

Developers may experience:

- Frustration and loss of focus
- Increased multitasking
- Reduced ownership and autonomy
- Lower quality
- Difficulty forecasting what they can complete

For the Scrum Team, repeated interruptions can gradually create a **reactive culture** where Sprint Planning becomes less valuable.

### 4. Questions the Scrum Master should ask first:

I'd explore the situation with questions such as:

**To the PO:**

- "What is causing these priority changes?"
- "Are these genuinely urgent, or simply higher priority?"
- "What happens if we don't address this item during the current Sprint?"
- "Does this change affect our Sprint Goal?"

**With the Developers:**

- "How are these changes affecting our ability to achieve the Sprint Goal?"
- "What work would need to stop if we take this item?"
- "Are we seeing technical or quality risks because of the interruptions?"

**With the entire Scrum Team:**

- "Is our Sprint Goal still valuable?"
- "Can we adjust Sprint Backlog scope while keeping the Sprint Goal intact?"
- "What working agreement would help us handle genuinely urgent requests?"

### 5. What the Scrum Master should avoid?

The Scrum Master should **not immediately**:

- Tell the PO, "You cannot change anything during a Sprint."
- Become the person who approves or rejects every priority change.
- Protect Developers from every business change.
- Automatically cancel the Sprint.
- Blame the PO or stakeholders.
- Treat velocity reduction as the primary problem.
- Force the team to complete every originally planned item regardless of changing circumstances.

### A strong Scrum Master approach:

The Scrum Master's role is to **facilitate transparency, help the team understand the impact of changes, and protect the Sprint Goal—not protect the original Sprint Backlog at all costs.**

A useful conversation is:

> **"We understand that priorities can change. Let's make the reason and impact transparent, then determine whether we can accommodate the change while still achieving the Sprint Goal."**

That demonstrates a much stronger Scrum mindset than simply saying **"the PO shouldn't change priorities during a Sprint."**

## ✅ 5. What AI Did Well?
- AI did not immediately blame the Product Owner and considered multiple possible causes.
- It correctly focused on the Sprint Goal rather than simply protecting the original Sprint Backlog.
- It highlighted the risks of frequent context switching and interruptions.
- It provided practical coaching questions for the Product Owner, Developers, and Scrum Team.
- It identified behaviors the Scrum Master should avoid, such as becoming the person who approves every priority change.
- It emphasized transparency, collaboration, and understanding the impact of changes.

## ⚠️ 6. What AI Missed / Areas to Validate?
- AI provides general guidance, but it does not know the team's actual organizational context.
- The Scrum Master still needs to understand whether the change is truly urgent and what impact it has on the Sprint Goal.
- AI cannot independently assess stakeholder relationships, team dynamics, or organizational pressure.
- Scrum Guide principles should be used to validate the recommendations.
- AI should support the Scrum Master's thinking, not replace professional judgment.

## 🧠 7. Scrum Master Analysis:
The AI response provides a useful starting point, but the Scrum Master should apply the recommendations based on the actual team context.

The key learning for me is that the Scrum Master should not simply try to prevent all changes during a Sprint. The focus should be on protecting the Sprint Goal while making changes, impacts, and trade-offs transparent.

Before taking action, I would:

- Understand why the priority is changing.
- Check whether the Sprint Goal is still valid.
- Discuss the impact of the change with the Product Owner and Developers.
- Help the Scrum Team determine whether scope can be adjusted while preserving the Sprint Goal.
- Facilitate collaboration rather than acting as a gatekeeper.
- Escalate systemic issues only when necessary.

The Scrum Master's role is therefore not to "protect the Sprint Backlog at all costs", but to help the Scrum Team remain focused on delivering value while responding appropriately to changing circumstances.

## 💡 8. Key Learning:
### 🌱 What I Learned From This Experiment?

1. AI can help structure a complex Scrum situation and provide multiple perspectives.
2. A good prompt should provide context without forcing AI toward a predetermined conclusion.
3. AI output still needs to be validated against Scrum principles and the actual team context.
4. The Scrum Master should focus on the Sprint Goal rather than simply protecting the original Sprint Backlog.
5. AI can support preparation for coaching conversations, but human judgment and facilitation remain essential.

## 🚀 9. Final Scrum Master Approach:

I would approach this situation through collaboration rather than immediately treating priority changes as a violation.

My approach would be:

1. Understand the reason behind the priority change.
2. Discuss the impact with the Product Owner and Developers.
3. Check whether the Sprint Goal is still relevant.
4. If the Sprint Goal remains valid, help the team determine whether scope can be adjusted.
5. If the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete, discuss the appropriate next step with the Product Owner.
6. Look for recurring patterns and address the underlying organizational or process issue through continuous improvement.

### 🔑 Key Takeaway:

> **The goal is not to prevent change. The goal is to help the Scrum Team respond to change while maintaining focus on the Sprint Goal and delivering value.**
